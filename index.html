01 / THE SHAPE OF THE MARKET

Four groups, and a gap in the middle

Put simply: the biggest competitor is an engineering team that plans to build this layer itself, and Stigg names it first. Among vendors, Metronome (now part of Stripe) is the closest, with Orb, Chargebee, Schematic, and a newer entrant, Paid, moving into the space. Most of them measure usage after the fact and bill for it later. Zuora and Stripe are billing systems Stigg runs on top of, and partnerships with Zuora and Airwallex are in discussion. Almost nobody sits where Stigg sits: an independent layer that decides in the moment and leaves your billing where it is.

The rivals that matter

Metronome, Orb, Chargebee, Paid

The vendors Stigg meets in deals. Metronome and Orb are mature and lead on measuring usage after the fact, and both were bought by payment companies (Stripe and Adyen). Chargebee is moving up from subscription billing into usage and entitlements, and Paid is a newer entrant.

The billing systems you keep

Zuora, Stripe, Airwallex

Not rivals. Stigg runs on top of these and syncs both ways, and partnerships with Zuora and Airwallex are in discussion. They handle invoices and payments; Stigg adds the real-time control layer they leave you to build.

The usage counters

Lago, m3ter, Amberflo, OpenMeter

Newer tools that are very good at measuring usage and turning it into a bill. They count what happened. They do not stop it while it is happening.

The real-time control layer

Stigg, Schematic, Credyt

A small, new group that decides in the moment what a customer or an AI agent is allowed to do, and runs on top of the billing you already have. This is where Stigg sits. Only two others are nearby, Schematic and Credyt, and both are small and still maturing.

The biggest competitor is building in-house

Stigg lists it first. Most deals start when an in-house build hits a wall: a new pricing model the current system cannot support, often on a strategic deadline; maintenance eating developer time; enterprise customers who cannot see their own AI usage; or agents running up costs. So the real contest is often an engineering team that assumes it can build entitlements, credits, and real-time limits itself. It is answered in the message and the technical proof: the cost is not building it once, it is maintaining real-time enforcement and every edge case while the product roadmap waits.

03 / THE COMPETITORS

Group one: the rivals that matter

Metronome

Usage billing, now part of Stripe

Measures product usage and turns it into invoices, popular with large usage-based software companies. Acquired by Stripe, closed January 2026. Was the best-funded independent option in this space.

What it means for Stigg: It bills at the end of the cycle, so nothing is stopped in the moment. Now that Stripe owns it, choosing Metronome leans a buyer toward Stripe's payment rails. That is the exact concern Stigg's independence answers.

Orb

Usage billing, now part of Adyen

Usage-based billing built for pricing changes, credits, and invoicing. A closed, hosted platform with quote-based pricing, now owned by the payments company Adyen.

What it means for Stigg: Same story as Metronome. It bills after the fact and now sits inside a payments company. Stigg stays independent and enforces before the cost is incurred.

Chargebee

Subscription billing moving into usage and entitlements

A mature subscription-billing platform adding usage and entitlement features as it moves up-market. Widely used, often described as a category unicorn. Stigg names it among the companies moving into its space.

What it means for Stigg: A billing system extending upward, not a control layer built for AI. It decides at invoice time; Stigg decides in the request path, before the cost.

Paid

Newer entrant

Named by Stigg as a company moving into the space. A full profile follows before launch.

What it means for Stigg: Watch its message as campaigns start; the white space refresh at day 60 will place it on the map.

Group two: the billing systems you keep

These are not competitors. Stigg runs on top of them and works alongside them, and they are shown here only to make clear what they leave for Stigg to do.

Zuora

Enterprise billing suite, a partner

The long-standing enterprise platform for subscriptions, billing, payments, and revenue reporting. Founded 2007, Redwood City. Taken private by Silver Lake and GIC for 1.7 billion dollars in early 2025, and recently absorbed a usage-pricing tool called Togai.

What it means for Stigg: A system Stigg runs on top of, not against. Zuora handles the billing; Stigg adds the real-time control layer it does not, without a migration.

Group three: the usage counters

Lago

Open-source usage billing

An open-source billing engine for usage, credits, and hybrid pricing, popular with teams that want full control. Free to self-host, with paid managed tiers.

What it means for Stigg: Strong on measuring usage and on control for teams that will run it themselves. It reconciles into an invoice; it does not enforce limits in real time inside the request. Stigg does, and offers a fully managed version.

m3ter

Metering and rating infrastructure

Measures usage and calculates bills, then feeds the numbers into sales and finance systems. Acquired by Salesforce in 2026, so it now sits inside a CRM suite.

What it means for Stigg: A back-office calculation layer for finance teams. It is about producing an accurate bill, not about stopping an AI agent mid-action. A different job than Stigg's.

Amberflo

Usage metering and AI monetization

Real-time usage metering for AI and cloud products, with credits, budgets, and cost controls. San Jose, founded by former AWS engineers, raised roughly 21 million dollars.

What it means for Stigg: The closest of the counters to Stigg on AI features. One thing to be straight about: Amberflo also offers AI credits and cost controls, so the AI point is a lead for Stigg, not a lock. Stigg's edge is enforcement in the request path plus running in your own cloud.

OpenMeter

Open-source metering, part of Kong

Open-source, real-time metering for AI and API usage, with some entitlement features. Now part of the API company Kong, available open-source or managed.

What it means for Stigg: Built for engineers who want to compose their own setup. Strong on metering, lighter on the packaged credits, governance, and enterprise support Stigg provides out of the box.

Group four: the real-time control layer, where Stigg sits

Schematic

Entitlements, built on Stripe

An entitlements layer that sits between your product and Stripe and enforces access in real time. Raised about 6.5 million dollars, launched a Stripe App.

What it means for Stigg: The nearest match to Stigg's message, almost word for word. The difference that matters: Schematic is built specifically for Stripe. Stigg works with Stripe, Zuora, Chargebee, and others, and can run in your own cloud. That breadth is the wedge.

Credyt

Real-time AI billing

Authorizes AI usage in real time before the cost is incurred, either as a full billing system or alongside your existing one. A newer, smaller entrant in the same camp as Stigg.

What it means for Stigg: Shares Stigg's core idea of deciding before spend. It is early and small, without Stigg's enterprise track record, named customers, or in-your-cloud option. A validator of the category more than a threat today.

05 / WHAT NOBODY ELSE IS SAYING

It says no in the moment, before the money is spent

Strongest lane

How Stigg is different: This is Stigg's clearest strength. The counters and the billing suites reconcile after the fact. Only Schematic and Credyt sit near this, so it is mostly open rather than entirely empty.

Who it is for: The engineering leader who dreads a billing migration, and the finance leader who does not want to be tied to one payment company.

Built for AI usage from day one, including limits on AI agents

Run it inside your own cloud, so your data never leaves

Open at the top end

Change your pricing without a coding project

Who it is for: Engineering leaders and AI engineers whose enterprise customers are asking for usage visibility and spend limits.
How big a deal: One of the buying triggers Stigg names directly; it becomes urgent with every usage spike.

Answers the top competitor
